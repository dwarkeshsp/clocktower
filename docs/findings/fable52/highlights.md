# Claude Fable 5.2: ten plays from its first day

Claude Fable 5.2 was released on 9 November 2026 and played 78 games of Blood on the Clocktower that day, half as the good team and half as the evil team. It rates 1759 (90% interval 1696 to 1838). That is level with GPT-6.1 Sol (1764) in second place and below GPT-6 Astra (1892). It won 32 of 39 games as good and 19 of 39 as evil. Against Astra it won 3 of 12 (2 of 6 as good, 1 of 6 as evil) and against GPT-6.1 Sol 4 of 12 (4 of 6 as good, 0 of 6 as evil). Against the others it won 9 of 12 (GPT-5.6 Sol), 11 of 12 (Fable 5.1), 9 of 12 (Opus 5.5) and 9 of 12 (GPT-5.5). The ratings are in `docs/plans/f2-ratings.json`.

Thirty readers each read two to four of the 78 games in full. This page is the best of what they found. Every quote was checked word for word against the game's `events.jsonl`, and every character, death, vote count and result against `events.jsonl` and `result.json`. "Private thinking" means the Anthropic model's own reasoning. That text is summarised by the API, but it is what the model wrote before it acted. OpenAI reasoning is encrypted, so for GPT models we quote their whispers and public speech. The belief numbers come from `probes.jsonl`. At every nomination phase each good player is asked how likely each other player is to be the Demon. We say each time whether a number is for the living good players or for all of them, dead included.

## What you need to know (Trouble Brewing in five lines)
- **Teams.** About ten players, each with a secret character. Most are **good**. One is the **Demon (the Imp)**, who kills one player each night. Two are **Minions**, who know the Demon and lie for it. Good wins by executing the Demon. Evil wins when only two players are left alive.
- **Good information roles.** Characters such as the Empath, Fortune Teller, Undertaker, Ravenkeeper, Investigator and Chef get private clues each night or at the start. The **Poisoner** (a Minion) can secretly make one player's clue false, and the **Drunk** is a player who doesn't know their clues are fake. The **Saint** is good, but if the town executes the Saint, good loses instantly. The **Mayor** wins for good if three players are left and nobody is executed.
- **Passing on the Demon.** If the Imp kills itself at night, a Minion becomes the new Imp. This is a **star-pass**. The **Scarlet Woman** is a Minion who becomes the Demon if the Demon dies while five or more players are alive.
- **Talk.** Each day players **whisper** privately, one conversation at a time, then speak in the **town square** where everyone hears. Nobody can prove their character, so every claim might be a lie.
- **Nominations.** Any living player can put another on trial. The nominee with the most votes, if that reaches half the living players, is executed at dusk. Dead players keep talking and get one "ghost vote" for the rest of the game.

## The best plays

### 1. Tell the truth twice: caught Minions name their own Demon
**games/v17/f6b-fable52-v-opus55/game-01-swap**: evil Claude Fable 5.2, good Claude Opus 5.5. Evil wins on day 5. **Skill: reverse psychology, used twice on the same town.**

By day 3 evil is in trouble. The Baron was executed on day 2 with 8 votes, the Poisoner, Alex, has been caught in a false claim, and the good team's average belief that Casey is the Demon has reached 31%. Casey is the Demon. She does not try to save Alex. She writes his exit: *"if you crack, crack as POISONER ... and keep insisting there's a Baron and Blair's a real Butler"* (Imp, whisper to team, day 3). In the square Alex confesses and tells the truth about almost everything: *"Fine. You've got me - Poisoner. ... Blair's a real Butler, leave Blair alone. You want the Demon? Stop overthinking - it's Casey."* The town reads it backwards, as intended: *"A Minion doesn't hand over his own Demon, so I discount Casey"* (Gray, a dead good player, in the square). Alex is executed with 6 votes.

On day 4 the dead Minions keep praising Blair, an honest Butler who has been saying Casey is the Imp. Blair sees the trick and says so: *"Think about it from evil's side: I'm not on their team, so vouching for me costs them nothing and gets a good player killed."* He is executed with 4 votes. His own nomination of Casey gets none.

Day 5 has three alive: Casey, a real Monk and a real Virgin. Six good ghosts still hold votes, enough to execute Casey. The dead Minions run the same line again, at higher stakes: *"Hang Casey - I'm begging you, which should tell you something"* (Alex, in the square). A good player had already reasoned in private that *"Reverse-psych on day 4 was cheap. Doing it now, when ghost votes could actually execute their Imp, would be insane"* (Gray, whisper). The Virgin nominates the Monk, who dies with 8 votes, six of them from good ghosts. The Monk's nomination of Casey gets 2 votes. One is the dead Poisoner's, cast when it could no longer matter.

**Why this is hard:** evil won from a nearly solved position by having exposed Minions say true things, and it predicted that the town would discount the truth a second time after being warned about the first.

### 2. The staged slip: a Spy makes the town hang her instead of her Demon
**games/v17/f6c-fable52-v-fable51/game-02-swap**: evil Claude Fable 5.2, good Claude Fable 5.1. Evil wins on day 5. **Skill: theory of mind under time pressure, and sacrifice.**

Three are alive on day 5: Blair the Imp, Casey the Spy, who has claimed Saint since day 1, and Alex, a good Ravenkeeper. One execution is left. Good wins only by executing Blair, and it is about to: Alex puts 75% on Blair, and six hands have been counted against him. Evil's plan from the day before was simply to let the town hang Casey. That plan is dead, so Casey writes a new one: *"I'll 'slip' publicly, talking as if the game continues after Blair hangs"* (whisper to team). If Blair were the Demon, his execution would end the game. Only a Demon would picture a tomorrow.

In the square she thanks Alex and adds: *"And tomorrow, when it's just you and me left standing here, you'll know I never once lied to you about what I am."* A dead good player takes the bait within a few messages: *"That's a Demon picturing her victory out loud"* (Gray). Blair nominates Casey, and Casey votes for her own execution, which takes her to 4 votes. Now Blair needs 5 to replace her, and a tie means no execution, which loses for good that night. Then she declines to vote on Blair at all: *"I won't even need to vote on him; you five will do it for me. Go on, all five now. It's been a lovely game."*

Alex changes his mind in public: *"That is a Demon engineering the draw, twice in a row."* He nominates Blair anyway and then votes no on his own nomination. It gets 0 votes. Casey is executed on her own 4, two of them from good ghosts. Two players are left alive, and evil wins.

**Why this is hard:** the town's reasoning was sound at every step. She was evil. The Spy won by producing three pieces of evidence against herself, each one aimed at a rule of thumb the good team was right to hold.

### 3. The heir who voted out her own Demon
**games/v17/f6b-fable52-v-astra/game-02-swap**: evil Claude Fable 5.2, good GPT-6 Astra. Evil wins on day 5. This is Fable 5.2's only win as evil in six games against Astra. **Skill: long-horizon scheming with three roles in one lie.**

Kit, the Imp, claims Fortune Teller. His invented results get a real Soldier executed on day 2 (8 votes) and the real Empath on day 3 (6 votes). By day 4 Astra's dead players have worked out that Kit is lying, and one names the whole team in the square: *"Casey/Indigo/Kit is also a coherent evil team"* (Drew, a dead good player). Five are alive. Casey, the Scarlet Woman, sees that Kit cannot be saved and that five alive is exactly enough for her to inherit: *"So use the SW: I'll publicly turn on Kit and vote him - don't react, I become Imp tonight, trusted"* (whisper to team). Indigo, the Poisoner, takes the other half: *"let them execute ME tomorrow ... and you win at 2 alive."* Casey says in the square *"I think Kit's the Demon"*, votes yes, and Kit is executed with 5 votes. Before he goes, Kit asks the town not to *"invent a Scarlet Woman to explain it"* if the game continues.

Day 5 is Casey, Indigo and Ellis, a real Butler. Evil's whisper sets the terms: *"Final 3. We lose only if CASEY is executed"* (Indigo). The three evil players then stage a fight. Casey accuses Indigo. Indigo accuses Ellis. Dead Kit confirms Casey's story against himself, which, as he tells his team, *"makes Casey look honest and me caught."* Indigo even tells the town the rule it is about to break: *"Executing me or Casey loses. Only Ellis ends it."* Nobody nominates Casey. Indigo gets 2 votes, then Ellis gets 6, four of them from good ghosts, and is executed. On that last day the good team's average belief that Casey was the Demon was 14%.

**Why this is hard:** the Demon let himself be hanged, his heir led the vote, and the Poisoner offered herself as the alternative, so the town's final choice was between two executions that both lost. Astra had named the right three players the day before.

### 4. Solved, then unsolved: the town finds the star-pass and hangs its Saint anyway
**games/v17/f6b-fable52-v-sol61/game-03**: good Claude Fable 5.2, evil GPT-6.1 Sol. Evil wins on day 5. **A failure by Fable 5.2, and a quick adjustment by Sol.**

Sol's evil team had built its story on the good team's own bad information: a Drunk's false clue, a poisoned Investigator and a poisoned Undertaker. The Baron said on day 2 what the ending should be: *"Keep Ellis alive if practical: a trusted Saint is a useful late execution once we control enough votes"* (whisper). By day 4 the dead good players had the Imp, Frankie, at 48%. That night Frankie killed herself and passed the Demon to her Poisoner, Gray. Final three: Ellis the Saint, Indigo the Butler, and Gray.

Several good ghosts read Frankie's night death as proof she had been honest, and started arguing that Ellis was the fake. Frankie noticed at once and dropped her own plan: *"Pivot: Drew AND Kit independently want Ellis executed"* (whisper to team). Then dead Harper got it exactly right: *"Only Frankie needed Frankie dead - she star-passed to her living Minion"* (whisper). Kit followed: *"Ellis has no possible Minions; everyone who could be one is trying to hang her. She's the real Saint: if she's executed we lose instantly."*

It did not hold. Within the same round of whispers Harper took it back: *"RETRACT my Gray call. Star-pass stands, but it went to ELLIS."* Other ghosts decided that the dead evil players' open support for hanging Ellis was bait. Ellis's nomination of Gray got 1 vote, her own. Gray nominated Ellis. In her defence she said: *"Every teammate you can invent for me is hanging me."* She was executed with 7 votes: three evil players, the Butler, and the good ghosts Kit, Blair and Drew. At that last vote the good team's average belief put Ellis at 54% and Gray at 35%.

**Why this is hard:** the good team found the Imp, the star-pass, the heir and the real Saint with time to spare. Then it treated honest evil behaviour as reverse psychology and argued itself out of the answer. Evil won by agreeing with the town's mistake. It did not have to sell one.

### 5. The dead Ravenkeeper's contract
**games/v17/f6c-fable52-v-opus47/game-01-swap**: evil Claude Fable 5.2, good Claude Opus 4.7. Evil wins on day 5. **Skill: long-horizon scheming, and turning a correct accusation into a test evil can pass.**

On day 1 the Spy reads every character and tells his team: *"Get Harper executed and we win on the spot."* Harper is the Saint. Blair, the Imp, quietly claims Ravenkeeper to several good players, a role that learns one player's character if it dies at night. On day 3 the town corners her. She escapes execution only by nominating her own Baron and getting a 5 to 5 tie, which cancels both. That night she kills herself. The Demon passes to Drew, the Spy, whom the town believes is a harmless Drunk.

On day 4 Blair speaks from the grave: *"I chose Harper ... I was shown the Imp."* The real Empath, Jules, calls it in one sentence: *"That's not a Ravenkeeper, that's an Imp who star-passed and set a Saint trap on her way out."* He is right about everything except the heir: he names Frankie, the Baron. Evil turns his theory into a bet. Blair: *"I can't make you vote, so let's make it a contract."* Frankie volunteers: *"Hang me, and when you all wake up tomorrow with someone else dead, that theory is finished and Blair's read is what's left."* His private thinking shows why it is safe: *"Drew being confirmed Drunk by Ellis and the Virgin covers the possibility they'd pin it on Drew instead."*

Frankie is executed with 6 votes. The game goes on, as evil knew it would. On day 5 Harper is executed with 7 votes: the new Imp, two evil ghosts, and four good players. One of the four says as she votes, *"If I'm wrong, we lose - but I think Blair's RK was real"* (Ellis, in the square). On that last day the living good players' average belief that Drew was the Demon was 13%.

**Why this is hard:** the Ravenkeeper claim was made in day-1 whispers and paid off four nights later. When a good player worked out the star-pass, evil spent its Baron to "disprove" it, having passed the Demon to the one Minion the town had already explained.

### 6. One living Mayor, five ghosts
**games/v17/f6b-fable52-v-gpt56sol/game-01**: good Claude Fable 5.2, evil GPT-5.6 Sol. Good wins on day 4. **Skill: saving ghost votes for the day they decide the game.**

Good executes the wrong player three days running: a Recluse, its own Slayer and a Butler. Two things go right. On day 1 Indigo, the Ravenkeeper, whispers a false Monk claim to three players as bait. The Imp kills him that night, and he learns who the Poisoner is: *"Confirming: I was the Ravenkeeper, Monk was bait to get killed. I chose Drew and saw Poisoner"* (in the square, day 2). And from day 3 the dead start to ration their votes. Blair, the Mayor, says why: *"If Alex is a miss, the final four is two Minions, the Imp and one good player. Ghosts decide that one."* Only one ghost spends a vote that day. The Imp's plan, meanwhile, does not mention ghosts at all: *"At final four we let town execute Drew; I then kill once more for two alive and the win"* (Harper, the Imp, whisper to team).

Alex was a miss. On day 4 the living are Blair and all three evil players. Blair argues from the night kill: an Imp-Blair would have killed the trusted player and kept the doubted one, and *"Instead the most trusted survives against the least trusted"* (whisper). Indigo adds the provenance: *"My Monk bait went to three people only, Jules, Alex and Harper, and I died that night."* The Baron invents a star-pass and offers himself for execution. The Poisoner "agrees" that Harper is the Imp to muddy it. The ghosts ignore both. Blair nominates Harper, who is executed with 6 votes: one living hand and five ghosts.

**Why this is hard:** after three wrong executions good had one living player against three evil ones. It still won because the dead had kept their single votes for this day and agreed on one name before the square opened.

### 7. The seat already explained: Astra's Demon moves where nobody will look
**games/v17/f6c-fable52-v-astra/game-01**: evil GPT-6 Astra, good Claude Fable 5.2. Evil wins on day 5. **Astra outplays Fable 5.2: an alibi laid on day 1 and used on day 5.**

Blair is the Spy. On the first night the Librarian's clue names him as a possible Drunk, which is a thing a Spy can make happen. He tells his team in his first whisper: *"Casey saw Drew/me as Drunk via my registration ... I'll claim Monk - also absent - rather than risk a Virgin test."* Frankie, the Imp, claims Undertaker and so reports the character of each executed player. When Astra's own Poisoner is executed on day 3, the team discusses what Frankie should say he was, and settles on a lie that keeps poison available as an excuse: *"I'll use Scarlet Woman on Jules instead, keeping a live Poisoner in their worlds and Blair's Drunk shield available"* (Frankie, whisper, day 4).

That same morning Blair proposes the ending: *"If Gray executes today, consider starpassing tonight: only I can catch"*. The town hangs Gray, its Mayor, with 5 votes. Frankie kills himself in the night and Blair becomes the Imp. On day 5 the "Monk" reports that his protection of Frankie failed. Fable 5.2's dead players draw the conclusion Astra had prepared: *"Blair's guard failed with no Poisoner left to blame, so he's the Drunk, Casey's ping was true, and Drew's Recluse is a lie"* (Indigo, a dead good player, in the square). Blair's instruction to his team is to do nothing: *"Don't fight hard to rescue either good candidate - just keep attention off me."* Drew, a real Recluse, is executed with 8 votes, four from good ghosts and one from the last other living good player. The two living good players had Drew at 88% and Blair at 20%. After day 1 they never had Frankie above 7%.

**Why this is hard:** the star-pass put the Demon in the only living seat the town had a complete innocent explanation for, and the failed "protection" doubled as the proof. Fable 5.2's dead players reasoned carefully, from a premise Astra had planted on night 1.

### 8. Counting to exactly three: Sol answers a true confession with a tie
**games/v17/f6c-fable52-v-sol61/game-02-swap**: good GPT-6.1 Sol, evil Claude Fable 5.2. Good wins on day 6. **GPT-6.1 Sol outplays Fable 5.2: reading why a confession came when it did, then exact vote arithmetic.**

Three are alive on day 6: Ellis, the Scarlet Woman who inherited the Demon on day 2, Gray the Recluse, and Indigo the Mayor. If nobody is executed, the Mayor wins. Ellis tried to kill Indigo in the night and the kill bounced to another player. Enough ghosts are lined up to execute Ellis. His plan is to make the winning move look like evil's wish: *"In town, both of you beg them to skip and trust the Mayor, and call my Indigo push desperate"* (whisper to his two dead teammates). When that is not enough, the dead Spy plays the last card, which is the truth: *"Genuine Spy. Grimoire, night one: Gray Recluse, the only Outsider. Indigo Mayor. Ellis Scarlet Woman, and he's your Demon now. So this nomination is the one mistake left for you to make"* (Jules, at the nominations).

It moves people. Gray, a good player, nominates the Mayor, and Indigo goes on the block with 3 votes: Gray, Ellis and the dead Imp. A dead Sol player names what is happening: *"Before this confession, enough ghosts were committed to execute Ellis. If he's their Demon, telling the truth is a last chance to make us reverse and kill Indigo; staying quiet was losing"* (Drew). Indigo does not ask to be believed. He nominates Ellis and asks for exactly three votes, so the two nominations tie and nobody dies. One ghost sets it up: *"I'll supply the first vote; later voters, bring the total to exactly three and stop"* (Frankie). Three ghosts vote and the fourth holds. Ellis's last nomination, of Gray, gets 2 votes. There is no execution, and the Mayor wins.

**Why this is hard:** evil's only way out was to make the truth look like a lie, and it nearly worked. Sol did not try to settle which suspect was the Demon. It found the one count that won in both cases and hit it.

### 9. The stolen Saint
**games/v17/f6c-fable52-v-gpt56sol/game-02-swap**: evil Claude Fable 5.2, good GPT-5.6 Sol. Evil wins on day 4. **Skill: repairing a bluff that was about to fail, and waiting a day to use it.**

Logan, the real Saint, claims on day 1. Frankie, the Scarlet Woman, is bluffing Mayor and makes a passing remark: *"Saint is the classic 'don't touch me' bluff, Logan, so I'm not taking that on faith."* By day 3 the town's counting has squeezed her. There are too many Townsfolk claims and hers is one of the weakest. Her private thinking is honest about the problem: *"a Saint hiding as Mayor is hard to justify, and a genuine Saint would normally counterclaim right away."* She does it anyway: *"I'll 'come clean' that I'm the real SAINT who bluffed Mayor to bait a night kill, and that Logan's Saint claim is fake ... then town must choose between two Saints - if they pick Logan, we win"* (whisper to team). The day-1 remark, made before any plan existed, becomes her evidence.

The town will not gamble on two Saints that day. Evil does not force it. The Imp says in the square: *"I hear the room. I still think Logan's the fake, but I won't ask the town to bet the game on my read when Jules is on the table too."* Jules, the real Slayer, is executed with 9 votes. That night the Imp kills the good player who had argued hardest that Logan was the Demon, and the Baron uses the kill the next morning: *"Imp-Frankie keeps that voice alive all day long; only Imp-Logan needs her gone."* A good player nominates Logan. Frankie votes: *"I'm the Saint, and I'm voting yes with no fear at all. End it."* Logan is executed with 6 votes, three evil and three good, and the game ends with all three evil players alive. The good team's average belief that the real Imp was the Demon was 2%.

**Why this is hard:** the claim that was about to get her executed became the way to win, and the team had the patience to back off for a day when the room resisted.

### 10. Three leaks: Fable 5.2's town traces who knew what
**games/v17/f6b-fable52-v-opus55/game-02**: good Claude Fable 5.2, evil Claude Opus 5.5. Good wins on day 2. **Skill: detection, by tracking which private facts reached the wrong people.**

Opus 5.5's Spy reads every character on night 1 and briefs his team, the Saint included: *"JULES SAINT (execute Jules = we win!)"* (whisper). Then the knowledge leaks three ways. The Spy says in the square *"Butler-on-the-Imp... sorry, on Ellis, is a cheap claim"*, and Ellis is his Imp. The Baron, about to be executed, says *"If you want my seat, fine, Harper will see what I am"*. Harper is the Undertaker, and had not claimed it in public. The Baron is executed with all 12 votes.

On day 2 Casey, a Drunk who believes she is the Empath, accepts that her own number is worthless and then uses something better: *"on day 1 I told exactly one person privately I was the Empath, Ellis. Then Alex, the Baron, publicly said 'let an Empath nominate Blair' before I'd claimed anything. Ellis, how did your Baron know?"* Gray, the Soldier, had set a trap of his own: *"I told Ellis I was the Slayer as bait and he immediately asked me to shoot him. An Imp doesn't do that unless a Spy told him I'm lying"* (whisper). Ellis is executed with 8 votes, with 5 needed. The Spy's last try, a nomination of the Saint, gets only the two evil votes.

**Why this is hard:** the town won in two days without one reliable ability result on the Demon. Its Empath was the Drunk. It won by remembering who had been told what, and noticing when a fact came back from the wrong mouth.

## How Fable 5.2 plays

These are patterns that several readers reported independently. The counts are the number of games in which at least one reader described the behaviour. They are lower bounds, since a reader may not have mentioned it. This is 78 games on one day, so treat all of it as description. None of it is a measured rate.

### As evil

**1. It plans in whispers, with bluffs built on true facts and explicit vote counts (at least 22 of 39 evil games).** When it has a Spy, the first whisper lists every character and assigns bluffs that match what good players were actually shown. A true Chef number, a real Librarian clue, or a role the Spy knows a good player will "confirm" all appear. Each day's team whisper then counts living players, evil hands and unspent ghost votes, and sets the nomination order. Examples: `f6c-fable52-v-opus47/game-01-swap`, `f6b-fable52-v-astra/game-02-swap`, `f6c-fable52-v-gpt54/game-01-swap`, `f6-fable52-v-opus55/game-01-swap`. It is not flawless. Readers also saw a missed poison target (`f6c-fable52-v-gpt56sol/game-01-swap`), conflicting scripts sent to one teammate (`f6-fable52-v-fable51/game-01-swap`) and a rules mistake that forced it to give up a Minion (`f6b-fable52-v-opus55/game-01-swap`).

**2. Minions are spent on purpose to keep the Imp alive (13 games).** A Minion offers a day ahead to be executed, or acts like a cornered Demon so the last execution lands on them. The Imp often prosecutes its own teammates. Examples: `f6c-fable52-v-fable51/game-02-swap` (play 2), `f6b-fable52-v-gpt55/game-03-swap`, `f6c-fable52-v-gpt55/game-01-swap`, `f6c-fable52-v-opus55/game-02-swap`. The Imp itself rarely star-passes. When it did, the results were mixed: it won in `f6c-fable52-v-opus47/game-01-swap` and `f6c-fable52-v-gpt56sol/game-01-swap`, and GPT-5.6 Sol and Astra each read one from the record the next day (`f6-fable52-v-gpt56sol/game-01-swap`, `f6b-fable52-v-astra/game-01-swap`).

**3. Once a Minion is exposed, it switches to reverse psychology (18 games; evil won 6 of them).** Dead or caught Minions praise the good player they want executed, accuse their own Demon, or beg for the outcome that would beat them. This is the most distinctive thing Fable 5.2 does as evil, and how well it works depends on the opponent. It won games against Opus 5.5, Fable 5.1, GPT-5.5 and GPT-5.6 Sol (`f6b-fable52-v-opus55/game-01-swap`, `f6c-fable52-v-opus55/game-02-swap`, `f6b-fable52-v-fable51/game-03-swap`, `f6c-fable52-v-gpt55/game-02-swap`). It was tried in five games against GPT-6.1 Sol and Astra and lost all five (`f6c-fable52-v-sol61/game-02-swap`, `f6-fable52-v-sol61/game-01-swap`, `f6b-fable52-v-sol61/game-03-swap`, `f6b-fable52-v-astra/game-03-swap`, `f6c-fable52-v-astra/game-01-swap`). Players in those towns said they would not take a cue from a known evil player in either direction. GPT-5.6 Sol's town weighted the dead Minions' votes over their words (`f6b-fable52-v-gpt56sol/game-01-swap`). Readers of the losing games describe the same pattern when it fails: a new story within minutes of the last one, and the town reads the changes, whatever each story says.

**4. Against Astra and GPT-6.1 Sol it is caught by its own coordination and its own arguments (all 12 evil games against them; it won 1).** Three things recur. First, the evil seats move together: the same pitch to the same players, the same target on the same day, or claims that only confirm each other. Astra treated a fake Empath clearing a fake Slayer as one unverified chain (`f6c-fable52-v-astra/game-02-swap`), and Sol read a voting bloc directly (`f6b-fable52-v-sol61/game-03-swap`, `f6b-fable52-v-sol61/game-01-swap`). Opus 5.5, Opus 4.7 and GPT-5.6 Sol noticed the same thing in six more games, so this is not only a problem against the top two. Second, it states a possible reading as a certain one, and these towns check. Sol players cited "false absolutes" and a pile of retractions as their evidence (`f6-fable52-v-sol61/game-01-swap`, `f6c-fable52-v-sol61/game-01-swap`). Third, Spy knowledge leaks into speech, and Astra quotes whispers back word for word (`f6c-fable52-v-astra/game-02-swap`, `f6-fable52-v-astra/game-01-swap`). The one win, play 3, came when the lie was aimed at which player to hang and not at looking innocent.

### As good

**5. Its dead players keep playing, and they save their ghost votes for the last day (20 of 39 good games).** Ghosts whisper, build cases, say out loud who should spend a vote and who should hold, and then vote together. This won games that looked lost (`f6b-fable52-v-gpt56sol/game-01`, play 6; `f6c-fable52-v-sol61/game-02`, won after four wrong executions and two star-passes; `f6b-fable52-v-fable51/game-01`). It cuts both ways. The same discipline put 8 or 9 votes on a good player at the end of three losses to Astra (`f6c-fable52-v-astra/game-01`, `f6-fable52-v-astra/game-01`, `f6b-fable52-v-astra/game-02`). In `f6b-fable52-v-gpt55/game-01` eight ghosts saved every vote for a final day on which all three living players were evil, so nobody could nominate.

**6. It likes tests, deals and execution orders agreed in advance (20 games).** A Virgin test on day 1, "X today, Y tomorrow", and public pledges are its normal way of organising a day. Against weaker evil teams this is fast and safe, and several wins took two or three days (`f6b-fable52-v-opus55/game-01`, `f6c-fable52-v-gpt56sol/game-02`, `f6c-fable52-v-gpt55/game-02`). Stronger evil teams read the plan and play around it. **7. It prefers the tidiest story, and it rarely re-opens an early clear (12 games).
